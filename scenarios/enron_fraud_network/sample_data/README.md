# enron_fraud_network sample data

Two files, both generated by `scripts/prepare_enron_fraud_network_dataset.py` (run it from
`services/training` with `uv run --with 'networkx>=3.4,<4' python ../../scripts/prepare_enron_fraud_network_dataset.py [--ablation]`;
two runs give byte-identical files):

- `enron_fraud_network.csv` — 143 Enron insiders, one row each: id (`person_id`), display
  columns (`name`, `role`), 25 label-free features and the label `poi`.
- `enron_network.json` — the network the Network tab draws (format:
  `libs/shared/src/ai_circus_shared/network_graph.py`): the 143 people, 100 anonymised
  colleagues who connect them, 11 curated entities, 2,992 e-mail ties with monthly
  counts (1999-01 .. 2002-06) and 29 cited relations.

## What the script does

- **Cleans the pay table.** Drops `TOTAL` (the spreadsheet's sum row), `THE TRAVEL AGENCY
  IN THE PARK` (a company — kept as a graph entity instead) and `LOCKHART EUGENE E` (no data
  at all), leaving 143 people and 18 persons of interest. Fixes the two rows ud120 shifted
  by one column (`BELFER ROBERT` right, `BHATNAGAR SANJAY` left — re-read from the PDF), then
  checks that every row's payments and stock components add up to their totals (they do,
  also after writing the CSV). A dash in the PDF means "no payment": missing money is 0.
  The pickle is only unpickled after its checksum matches *and* an opcode scan confirms it
  references no Python object at all (plain dicts only).
- **Adds money ratios**: bonus ÷ salary, the share of stock value cashed out by exercising
  options, stock value ÷ cash payments.
- **Builds the company e-mail network** with networkx: messages de-duplicated across mailbox
  folders (250,136 distinct, 1999-01 .. 2002-06), internal addresses only, broadcasts to more
  than 50 people excluded from ties; a *tie* is a pair who e-mailed each other both ways
  (4,448 people, 43,766 ties). Per person, through their ud120 e-mail address: e-mails sent
  and received, two-way contacts, PageRank and betweenness percentiles (betweenness sampled
  over 1,000 seeded pivots), contact clustering, the share of sent e-mail in Aug–Dec 2001
  and outside office hours (Houston time).
- **Removes leaks.** ud120's `from_poi_to_this_person`, `from_this_person_to_poi` and
  `shared_receipt_with_poi` count e-mails with people *already known* to be persons of
  interest — computed from the label — so they are dropped. So is "is this a corpus
  custodian" (FERC chose whose mailboxes to collect) and the e-mail address itself.
- **Writes the graph.** The 100 colleagues outside the pay table with the strongest two-way
  ties to at least two people in it are kept to connect them, anonymised ("Unscored
  colleague", `ctx-001` …; their ids follow address order but the addresses are not
  shipped). Ties with at least 3 messages are drawn (strongest 3,000). Communities are
  Louvain (seeded) on the drawn e-mail graph.

## What the numbers say (`--ablation`: 5-fold CV repeated 10×, training's own pipeline)

| Inputs | Logistic regression | LightGBM (small data) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Money only (pay + stock + ratios) | 0.739 ± 0.119 | 0.821 ± 0.091 |
| Network only (label-free) | 0.720 ± 0.087 | 0.732 ± 0.124 |
| **Money + network (what the scenario trains on)** | 0.746 ± 0.118 | 0.818 ± 0.081 |
| Money + ud120's e-mail counts (leak the label) | 0.726 ± 0.120 | 0.803 ± 0.089 |
| Diagnostic: "has any e-mail trail" flag alone | 0.628 ± 0.031 | 0.628 ± 0.031 |
| Money + network, only the 111 people with an e-mail trail | 0.673 ± 0.155 | 0.725 ± 0.116 |

- Pay carries most of the signal; the e-mail network alone reaches ~0.73 without any label
  knowledge, and adds little on top of pay — an honest result on 18 positives.
- The spread is large: with 18 positives a single hold-out holds ~4 of them.
- **Sampling bias to disclose**: every person of interest has some e-mail trail, while 32
  others have none — mostly non-employee directors (no Enron mailbox), partly the way the
  corpus was collected. That flag alone is worth AUC 0.63, and restricted to the 111 people
  *with* a trail the model drops to ~0.73. `crisis_activity_share` and e-mail volumes also
  depend on whose mailbox FERC collected and how long it was kept.

## Reading it responsibly

A person of interest is someone indicted, who settled with the SEC (usually without
admitting wrongdoing) or who testified in exchange for immunity — not a list of convictions.
The model scores resemblance to their pay-and-e-mail profile; a high score for anyone else
is a model false positive, not an accusation. The scenario's chat context and the Network
tab say so.

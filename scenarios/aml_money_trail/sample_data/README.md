# aml_money_trail sample data

Two files, both generated by `scripts/prepare_aml_money_trail_dataset.py` (run it from
`services/training` with `uv run python ../../scripts/prepare_aml_money_trail_dataset.py [--diagnostics] [--ablation]`;
the raw files are cached in `~/.cache/ai-circus/aml/`; two runs give byte-identical files):

- `aml_money_trail.csv` — 12,253 account holders, one row each: id (`holder_id`, the
  generator's entity id), display columns (`name`, `home_bank`, `home_country`, `typology`,
  `attempt_id`), 28 label-free behaviour features and the label `laundering`.
- `aml_money_trail_network.json` — the network the Money Trail tab draws (format:
  `libs/shared/src/ai_circus_shared/network_graph.py`, with **hourly** periods
  `2022-09-01T00 … 2022-09-10T23`): a *case slice* of 1,000 holders (rows), 498 holders
  outside the consortium (context), the 13 member banks (entities) and 6,033 edges — `holds`
  (holder → member bank, weight = accounts) and one flow edge per payer, payee and payment
  format (ACH, wire, cheque, credit card, cash, Bitcoin) carrying the USD moved per hour.

## Source (pinned by SHA-256)

Blanuša, Atasu, *Realistic Synthetic Financial Transactions for Anti-Money Laundering Models*,
NeurIPS 2023 Datasets & Benchmarks ([arXiv:2306.16424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16424)),
released under the **Community Data License Agreement – Sharing 1.0** (Kaggle
`ealtman2019/ibm-transactions-for-anti-money-laundering-aml`). The files come from the Hugging
Face mirror `OsamaMIT/IBM-AML-HI-Small` at revision `95aa8c4`:

| File | sha256 | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `HI-Small_accounts.csv` | `78680852…` | 518,581 bank accounts → holder (`Entity ID`, `Entity Name`) and bank |
| `HI-Small_Trans.csv` | `b19d39f5…` | 5.08 M transfers, 1–18 Sep 2022, with the label `Is Laundering` |
| `HI-Small_Patterns.txt` | `2c546b5c…` | the 3,209 laundering transfers that follow one of 8 typologies, grouped in 370 attempts |

**Everything is synthetic.** A generator simulates individuals, companies and banks, injects
laundering schemes (placement, layering, integration) and follows the illicit funds through every
hop, so each transfer is labelled. Every holder is a generated name ("Corporation #33520",
"Sole Proprietorship #50438") and every bank a generated one ("Spain Bank #439"); nothing refers
to a real person or company. CDLA-Sharing-1.0 applies to these derived files too: reuse them
under the same terms and keep the attribution.

## What the script does

- **Cuts the window.** Transfers dated after 10 Sep 2022 (1,108 of 5.08 M) are dropped from
  features, labels and graph: the generator's tail after the primary period is **59 %
  laundering** against 0.09 % overall, so keeping it would leak the label through timing.
- **Resolves accounts by (bank, number).** Eight account numbers exist at two banks each, and the
  transfer file zero-pads bank ids (`010` = bank `10`); accounts, transfers and patterns are all
  joined on that pair. Every transfer resolves.
- **Picks the consortium**: the biggest bank of each country, biggest first, until their holders
  reach 12,000 — 13 banks in the United States, Japan, China, India, Germany, the United Kingdom,
  Switzerland, Finland, Australia, France, Mexico, Canada and Israel. Crypto exchanges are never
  members. A *row* is a holder with an account at a member bank (holders typed
  `Country` or `Direct` are left out).
- **Keeps what the consortium can see**: transfers between *distinct* holders that touch a member
  bank (568,208 of 4.42 M). Moves between a holder's own accounts (mostly "Reinvestment") are left
  out.
- **Label** `laundering` = the holder sent or received at least one laundering transfer the
  consortium can see — **448 of 12,253 (3.7 %)**. It is involvement, not guilt: receivers can be
  unwitting mules. **440 further holders launder only through transfers the consortium cannot see**
  and are labelled 0 — the limit of a partial view, kept on purpose.
- **Features** (28, all from behaviour): holder type, accounts / banks / countries, transfers sent
  and received, USD sent / received / largest (each currency at the median rate implied by the
  dataset's own cross-currency transfers), pass-through ratio, median dwell hours (−1 = money
  never moved on), payees / payers, peak daily fan-out / fan-in, reciprocity, payment-format
  shares, cross-border share, currencies used, night share, peak-day share, PageRank percentile
  and 3-hop return cycles (both over every visible holder). `--diagnostics` prints each feature's
  single-feature AUC: the best is 0.685, so no feature leaks the label.
- **Display-only columns**: `typology` and `attempt_id` say which of the 8 laundering shapes (and
  which attempt) a positive holder belongs to (`Unpatterned` = laundering outside the 8 shapes,
  `—` = none). They are never model inputs: the Money Trail tab shows them on reveal.
- **Writes the graph.** 10 attempts of every typology (round-robin while the slice stays within
  its budget) with all the holders they touch, plus the legitimate holders those trade with;
  banks as entities; outsiders as context. Sampling bias to disclose: the slice
  over-represents laundering (it is built around attempts), so the map is a case file, not a
  random sample — the KPIs and the backtest use all 12,253 rows.

## What the numbers say (`--ablation`: 5-fold CV repeated 3×, training's own pipeline)

| Inputs | Model | ROC AUC | PR AUC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Everything (what the scenario trains on)** | logistic regression | 0.798 ± 0.026 | 0.304 ± 0.053 |
| **Everything (what the scenario trains on)** | LightGBM | 0.838 ± 0.015 | 0.308 ± 0.037 |
| Without the payment-format mix (ACH artefact) | logistic regression | 0.789 ± 0.022 | 0.294 ± 0.046 |
| Without the payment-format mix (ACH artefact) | LightGBM | 0.812 ± 0.021 | 0.289 ± 0.039 |
| Flow behaviour only (no profile, format mix or network) | logistic regression | 0.756 ± 0.029 | 0.231 ± 0.037 |
| Flow behaviour only (no profile, format mix or network) | LightGBM | 0.764 ± 0.021 | 0.233 ± 0.045 |
| Network position only | logistic regression | 0.655 ± 0.027 | 0.183 ± 0.038 |
| Network position only | LightGBM | 0.648 ± 0.022 | 0.159 ± 0.029 |

- The base rate is 3.7 %, so a PR AUC of ~0.31 is an ~8× lift over chance.
- Behaviour alone (dwell time, fan-in/fan-out, pass-through, bursts) already reaches ~0.76; the
  payment-format mix adds ~0.03 and network position alone reaches ~0.65.

## Reading it responsibly

- **Generator artefacts a real bank would not see.** About 87 % of laundering transfers go by ACH
  against 12 % of all transfers; laundering never changes currency (98.6 % of all transfers
  don't either); and the schemes follow eight textbook shapes. Quote the AUC as a property of
  this simulation.
- **A partial view.** The consortium only sees transfers that touch a member bank; laundering
  chains disappear from its view whenever they cross a non-member bank (the 440 holders above).
- **A score is not an accusation.** It measures resemblance to the behaviour of holders the
  simulation's laundering ran through — for a case analyst to review, never a conclusion.

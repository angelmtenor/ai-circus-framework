# global_health_shipments sample data

`global_health_shipments.csv` — 9,949 line items of HIV/AIDS medicines and test kits
shipped by the PEPFAR Supply Chain Management System (SCMS) to 42 countries between
2006 and 2015, one row each, generated by
`scripts/prepare_global_health_shipments_dataset.py`.

**Source:** USAID, *Supply Chain Shipment Pricing Data* (data.usaid.gov dataset
`a3rc-nmf6`, also listed on catalog.data.gov) — data produced by the US federal
government, **public domain**; USAID is credited as the source. Not endorsed by USAID.
data.usaid.gov is offline since 2025, so the script fetches the export
(`SCMS_Delivery_History_Dataset.csv`, 10,324 rows) from two independent GitHub mirrors
pinned by commit, and refuses to build unless the file hashes to the reviewed SHA-256
(`918b992d…8673`; both mirrors are byte-identical).

What the script does:

- labels `delivered_late = 1` when *Delivered to Client Date* is after *Scheduled
  Delivery Date* (11.9% of rows; median 12 days late);
- keeps only inputs known when the shipment is planned — destination, fulfilment route,
  shipment mode, product group / sub-classification, dosage form (17 forms folded into
  6), first-line designation, quantity, value, pack and unit price, line items in the
  same shipment (same ASN/DN), planned lead time (scheduled date minus the price quote
  sent to the client, or minus the purchase order when no quote was recorded, else the
  median of its fulfilment route — `lead_time_basis` says which), scheduled year and
  month. Freight cost, insurance, weight and the delivery dates themselves are *not*
  model inputs;
- carries display-only columns for the UI's globe: item description, vendor,
  manufacturing site with hand-mapped city-level coordinates, the destination's
  capital as its map anchor, scheduled and delivered dates, days late;
- drops 17 rows whose manufacturing site has no location on record ("Not Applicable",
  "INVERNESS ANY", …) and 360 rows with no shipment mode.

Caveats: the scheduled date is the one on record at delivery — if some were revised
after a delay, a long planned lead time partly reflects that; the programme's
performance shifts strongly by year (2010-2011 peak), so the scheduled year is a model
input and the model describes this programme's history rather than any future one.
